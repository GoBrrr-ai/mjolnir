# Nvidia DGX
DGX  
The product line is intended to bridge the gap between GPUs and[AI accelerators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_accelerator "AI accelerator")using specific features for deep learning workloads.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_DGX#cite_note-4)The initial Pascal-based DGX-1 delivered 170[teraflops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teraflop "Teraflop")of[half precision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FP16 "FP16")processing,[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_DGX#cite_note-5)while the Volta-based upgrade increased this to 960[teraflops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teraflop "Teraflop").[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_DGX#cite_note-6)
#### DGX Station
The DGX station was first available with the following specifications.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_DGX#cite_note-10)
* 480 TFLOPS FP16
The DGX-2 differs from other DGX models in that it contains two separate GPU daughterboards, each with eight GPUs. These boards are connected by an NVSwitch system that allows for full bandwidth communication across all GPUs in the system, without additional latency between boards.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_DGX#cite_note-:1-16)
#### DGX A100 Server
Announced and released on May 14, 2020. The DGX A100 was the 3rd generation of DGX server, including 8[Ampere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ampere_\(microarchitecture\) "Ampere \(microarchitecture\)")-based A100 accelerators.[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_DGX#cite_note-anand-A100-21)Also included is 15TB of[PCIe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PCI_express "PCI express")gen 4[NVMe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NVMe "NVMe")storage,[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_DGX#cite_note-verge-A100-22)1TB of RAM, and eight[Mellanox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mellanox "Mellanox")-powered 200GB/s HDR InfiniBand ConnectX-6[NICs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_interface_controller "Network interface controller"). The DGX A100 is in a much smaller enclosure than its predecessor, the DGX-2, taking up only 6 Rack units.[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_DGX#cite_note-23)
The DGX A100 also moved to a 64 core[AMD EPYC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epyc "Epyc")7742 CPU, the first DGX server to not be built with an Intel Xeon CPU. The initial price for the DGX A100 Server was $199,000.[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_DGX#cite_note-anand-A100-21)
#### DGX Station A100
As the successor to the original DGX Station, the DGX Station A100, aims to fill the same niche as the DGX station in being a quiet, efficient, turnkey[cluster-in-a-box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_cluster "Computer cluster")solution that can be purchased, leased, or rented by smaller companies or individuals who want to utilize machine learning. It follows many of the design choices of the original DGX station, such as the tower orientation, single socket CPU[mainboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motherboard "Motherboard"), a new refrigerant-based cooling system, and a reduced number of accelerators compared to the corresponding rackmount DGX A100 of the same generation.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_DGX#cite_note-nvidia.com-13)The price for the DGX Station A100 320G is $149,000 and $99,000 for the 160G model, Nvidia also offers Station rental at ~US$9000 per month through partners in the US (rentacomputer.com) and Europe (iRent IT Systems) to help reduce the costs of implementing these systems at a small scale.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_DGX#cite_note-24)[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_DGX#cite_note-25)
The DGX Station A100 comes with two different configurations of the built in A100.
#### DGX H100 Server
Announced March 22, 2022[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_DGX#cite_note-26)and planned for release in Q3 2022,[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_DGX#cite_note-27)The DGX H100 is the 4th generation of DGX servers, built with 8[Hopper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopper_\(microarchitecture\) "Hopper \(microarchitecture\)")-based H100 accelerators, for a total of 32 PFLOPs of FP8 AI compute and 640GB of HBM3 Memory, an upgrade over the DGX A100s 640GB HBM2 memory. This upgrade also increases[VRAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_random_access_memory "Video random access memory")bandwidth to 3TB/s.[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_DGX#cite_note-Walton-28)The DGX H100 increases the[rackmount](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/19-inch_rack "19-inch rack")size to 8U to accommodate the 700W TDP of each H100 SXM card. The DGX H100 also has two 1.92TB SSDs for[Operating System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operating_system "Operating system")storage, and 30.72TB of[Solid state storage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid-state_drive "Solid-state drive")for application data.
One more notable addition is the presence of two Nvidia[Bluefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_BlueField "Nvidia BlueField")3[DPUs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_processing_unit "Data processing unit"),[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_DGX#cite_note-29)and the upgrade to 400Gbit/s InfiniBand via[Mellanox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mellanox_Technologies "Mellanox Technologies")ConnectX-7[NICs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_interface_controller "Network interface controller"), double the bandwidth of the DGX A100. The DGX H100 uses new 'Cedar Fever' cards, each with four ConnectX-7 400GB/s controllers, and two cards per system. This gives the DGX H100 3.2Tbit/s of fabric bandwidth across Infiniband.[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_DGX#cite_note-30)
The DGX H100 has two Xeon Platinum 8480C Scalable CPUs (Codenamed[Sapphire Rapids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapphire_Rapids_\(microprocessor\) "Sapphire Rapids \(microprocessor\)"))[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_DGX#cite_note-31)and 2 Terabytes of[System Memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random-access_memory "Random-access memory").[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_DGX#cite_note-32)
The DGX H100 was priced at £379,000 or ~US$482,000 at release.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_DGX#cite_note-33)
#### DGX GH200
Announced May 2023, the DGX GH200 connects 32 Nvidia Hopper Superchips into a singular superchip, that consists totally of 256 H100 GPUs, 32 Grace Neoverse V2 72-core CPUs, 32 OSFT single-port ConnectX-7 VPI of with 400Gbit/s InfiniBand and 16 dual-port[BlueField-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_BlueField "Nvidia BlueField")VPI with 200Gbit/s of[Mellanox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mellanox_Technologies "Mellanox Technologies")[](https://resources.nvidia.com/en-us-dgx-gh200/nvidia-dgx-gh200-datasheet-web-us)[](https://resources.nvidia.com/en-us-dgx-gh200/nvidia-grace-hopper-superchip-datasheet). Nvidia DGX GH200 is designed to handle terabyte-class models for massive recommender systems, generative AI, and graph analytics, offering 19.5TB of shared memory with linear scalability for giant AI models.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_DGX#cite_note-34)
#### DGX Helios
Announced May 2023, the DGX Helios supercomputer features 4 DGX GH200 systems. Each is interconnected with Nvidia Quantum-2 InfiniBand networking to supercharge data throughput for training large AI models. Helios includes 1,024 H100 GPUs.
#### DGX GB200
Nvidia DGX B200 8 way GPU Board (air cooled)
Announced March 2024,[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_DGX#cite_note-35)GB200 NVL72 connects 36 Grace Arm Neoverse V2 72-core CPUs and 72 B200 GPUs in a rack-scale design.[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_DGX#cite_note-36)The GB200 NVL72 is a liquid-cooled, rack-scale solution that boasts a 72-GPU NVLink domain that acts as a single massive GPU.[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_DGX#cite_note-37)Nvidia DGX GB200 offers 13.5TB HBM3e of shared memory with linear scalability for giant AI models, less than its predecessor DGX GH200.
#### DGX SuperPod
The DGX Superpod is a high performance turnkey[supercomputer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercomputer "Supercomputer")system provided by Nvidia using DGX hardware.[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_DGX#cite_note-38)It combines DGX compute nodes with fast storage and high bandwidth[networking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_network "Computer network")to provide a solution to high demand machine learning workloads. The[Selene supercomputer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selene_\(supercomputer\) "Selene \(supercomputer\)"), at the[Argonne National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argonne_National_Laboratory "Argonne National Laboratory"), is one example of a DGX SuperPod-based system.
#### DGX Spark
NVIDIA DGX Spark with[ConnectX-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mellanox_Technologies "Mellanox Technologies")NIC
The DGX Station is an upcoming Deskside AI Supercomputer based on the GB300 superchip - a Blackwell GPU and a Grace CPU, consisting of 72 Neoverse V2 ARM cores.[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_DGX#cite_note-47)These machines are targeted at AI researchers, programmers and for other users of AI technology, and have 748 GB of integrated RAM, making it possible to train or fine-tune fairly large models ("up to 1 trillion parameters" with quantization). 252 GB of these 748 GB of memory are HBM3e memory connected to the Blackwell GPU at 7.1 TB/s memory bandwidth. Several partner manufacturers offer versions of the DGX Station. The first deliveries to a broader user base will start from August of 2026, the price is cited as between $100.000 to $120.000 (as of July 2026), depending on the vendor and storage and GPU options.[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_DGX#cite_note-48)[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia_DGX#cite_note-49)
