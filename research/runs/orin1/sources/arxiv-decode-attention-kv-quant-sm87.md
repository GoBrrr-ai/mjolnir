# arXiv research brief — decode-phase attention kernels & KV-quantized decode for Jetson AGX Orin (sm_87)

**Date:** 2026-10-23 · **Requester context:** batch-1 decode, GQA 24:4, head_dim=256, paged KV (fp8 e4m3 / packed fp6 e2m3, per-head scales), L=4K–128K. ## Q1 — Split-KV / flash-decoding line (summary; full detail in sibling brief)

| Paper | Split-count / CTA recipe | Occupancy target |
|---|---|---|
| **2311.01282** FlashDecoding++ (Hong, Dai et al.) | Split KV; **asynchronized softmax** (declared max φ → partials independent, no cross-CTA sync; fallback path if x−φ out of window, >99.99% in-range) | Fills SMs with split partials; removes the sync that strands SMs |
| **2405.10480** LeanAttention (Microsoft) | **stream-K over KV tokens**, not fixed splits; unequal tiles equalize work; **catch-up reduction** (associative rescale) merges; split count pinned to ~#SMs with work-stealing | ≈100% SM wave at every L; combine overhead constant |
| **2501.01005** FlashInfer | Attention-State ⊕ (O, LSE) algebra; compile-time tiles + runtime load-balanced plan; persistent CTAs = k×#SMs; PDL overlap | k CTAs/SM persistent; workspace 2×CTA×T_q×H×(D+1) |
| **2404.00242** DeFT | Flatten tree/spec KV into even chunks + bitmask causality; two-stage partial + LSE reduce | balanced partials, ~0 mask IO |
| **2408.04093** Tree Attention | LSE merge as log-domain tree reduction | — |

**Recipe for latency-bound decode on a small GPU (synthesis):** batch-1 decode at long L is *occupancy*-bound before it is bandwidth-bound: with 16 SMs you need ≥32 CTAs (2/SM) all streaming KV; fixed per-request splits either strand SMs (too few) or blow up merge cost (too many). Pin total partials ≈ 2×16, size tiles by the smem budget (LeanTile: smallest efficient KV tile ≈ 256 tokens @d=64 / 128 @d=128 on A100-class → byte-equivalent 64–128 fp8 rows @d=256 on 100 KB Orin smem), equalize *bytes per SM* across the wave (stream-K), keep the inner loop sync-free (declared max or per-CTA online softmax only), and merge via a balanced LSE tree (merge is launch-bound: fewer partials is the only lever — matches our run-2 Thor finding).

## Q2 — Tensor-core vs GEMV decode at batch 1 (explicit claims)

- **2503.18773 — BitDecoding (Du, Cao, Cheng, Mai, Cao, Yang, 2025) — THE claim.** *"Existing systems decode inefficiently by relying solely on CUDA cores, underutilizing Tensor Cores."* *"GQA/MQA share a group of keys across multiple queries, enabling Tensor Cores to accelerate dot products."* Kernel mechanism: **layout-preserving low-bit packing** — because `ldmatrix`'s thread-to-register map is the TC interleaved fragment layout, quantizing/packing *in that layout* means dequant lands directly in TC registers, no global reshape; **warp-level dequant parallelism** (CUDA cores dequant while TCs matmul) in a **software-pipelined** producer–consumer kernel; `cp.async.ca` for packed KV (fine byte alignment), `ldmatrix` smem→reg with XOR-sizzling bank-conflict-free access. Results: **4.8× over FP16 FlashDecoding-v2 on Ampere** (kernel), 7.5× avg across Blackwell/Hopper/Ada, 3× single-batch 128K decode latency, 4.3× over QServe. This is a documented **TC-decode-beats-GEMV-decode** result at batch 1 for GQA long-context — the win comes exactly because decode is bandwidth-bound: dequant ALU work rides on otherwise-idle CUDA cores while TCs keep the mma pipe fed and the byte stream saturated.
- **2311.01282 — FlashDecoding++ (flat-GEMM §).** Wasted-lane analysis: engines padding M to 64 waste **>50% of mma work** at decode's M≪64; fix = **M-tile = 8** (pad Q rows to the mma M). Dataflow crossover measured on A100: **at M=1 a CUDA-core GEMV beats the TC path by ~22% (TC = 82% of CUDA-core); at M=4 TC is ~2× the CUDA-core path** → documented crossover between M=1 and M=4; pick per-M via profiled inflection. (Our verify shape is M=1..8 → TC path for M≥2, GEMV only if it wins at M=1 with a high-occupancy split.)
- **2405.04532 — QServe.** *"The efficiency of LLM serving on GPUs is critically influenced by operations on low-throughput CUDA cores"*; **1 CUDA-core op ≈ 50 INT4-TC ops on A100**; naive KV4 attention is **1.2× slower on A100** (CUDA-core roofline turns early) yet **1.7× faster on L40S** — i.e., GEMV-style dequant-on-CUDA-cores decode becomes the bottleneck on datacenter parts; the fix keeps fused attention *memory-bound* and dequant in registers.
- **2507.15465 — Rethinking LLM Inference Bottlenecks (Yun et al., 2025).** Roofline analysis: MHA/GQA decode attention has arithmetic intensity ~1–2 Op/B, far below any ridge point → memory-bound; nothing about FMA-vs-MMA changes the bytes; the kernel's job is to be a perfect byte pump. (Supports: at hd256 GQA 24:4, mma geometry is irrelevant to *bytes*; it matters only for keeping issue rate off the critical path — which is exactly the current kernel's failure mode.)
- **2410.02367 / 2411.10958 — SageAttention 1/2.** Ampere-proven quantized attention: INT8 QKᵀ mma (per-block scales, applied *outside* the mma: S = ÂB̂·δδ) + **FP16 PV with FP16 accumulators** (2× vs FP32-acc — "speedup only on RTX 4090 and 3090", i.e., Ampere-class); SageAttention2 moves to **INT4 QK at per-thread granularity** (each thread owns exactly one scale → zero extra dequant latency) + FP8 P̃V (Ada/Hopper; documents that fp8 mma's accumulator is effectively FP22 → two-level accumulation into an FP32 buffer per block). 481 TOPS on 4090 ≈ 3× FA2.
- **2407.08608 — FlashAttention-3.** FP8 attention with incoherent processing (Hopper WGMMA/TMA; warp-specialization, matmul↔softmax interleave). Hopper-only; the transferable ideas are asynchrony structure, not the fp8 mma (sm_87 has no fp8 TCs).

**Bottom line for sm_87 hd256 GQA 24:4 batch-1:** no paper claims GEMV is *generally* better; the only pro-GEMV datapoint is FlashDecoding++'s M=1 A100 result (and even there it's a 22% gap at *high* split occupancy). For GQA 24:4 the six Q rows per KV head fill an m16n8k16 M-tile to 6/8 (25% pad) — BitDecoding's GQA argument and its 4.8× Ampere number say the TC path with in-flight dequant is the right default; the current kernel's 9% BW is a *scheduling/occupancy* failure, not an FMA-vs-MMA choice per se (FMA decode at 8.3% occupancy cannot issue enough loads to saturate LPDDR5).

## Q3 — KV-quantized decode kernels: structures & numbers

| Paper | KV format / scales | Decode kernel structure | Reported tradeoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| **2402.02750 KIVI** (Liu et al.) | 2-bit; **K per-channel, V per-token** (channel-outlier analysis); K split into grouped (quantized) + residual (fp16) | **Dequant fused into the matmul** ("Q_MatMul") in CUDA; group-quant kernels in Triton; tiled matmul combines grouped+residual | 2.6× peak-memory ↓, 4× batch ↑, 2.35–3.47× throughput; ~no accuracy loss (LongBench −0.6% @2-bit) |
| **2405.04532 QServe / QoQ** (Lin, Tang, Yang… Song Han) | W4A8KV4; KV4 **dynamic per-head** scales; **SmoothAttention** = per-channel K outlier smoothing with the inverse scale moved to the (unquantized) Q | Fused decode attention with **in-register dequant** (register-level parallelism; subtraction-after-multiplication unpack order), compute-aware reordering; keeps kernel memory-bound (delays CUDA-core roofline turning point); naive INT4→FP dequant = 5 ALU ops (mask/shift/cvt/mul/sub) — their packing cuts effective overhead | KV4: 1.7× on L40S vs KV8 TRT-LLM (naive KV4 = 1.2× *slower* on A100 → kernel structure matters as much as format); serving throughput 1.2–2.4× (A100), 1.4–3.5× (L40S) |
| **2401.18079 KVQuant** (Hooper et al.) | 4-bit/3-bit; per-channel **pre-RoPE** K; non-uniform datatypes (NUQ+); per-vector **dense-and-sparse** outlier isolation | Custom CUDA decode kernels (llama.cpp): dequant-in-flight + GEMV; memory-bound speedup | ~**1.7× vs fp16 GEMV decode**; 1M ctx on one A100; <0.1 ppl degradation @3-bit |
| **2405.06219 SKVQ** | 2-bit K / 1.5-bit V; **channel reordering** to cluster outliers + clipped dynamic group quant; recent-window tokens fp16 | Dequant + matmul fused; sliding-window high-precision path | "up to 7× faster decoding"; 1M ctx on 80 GB |
| **2405.14256 ZipCache** | 4-bit; **channel-separable tokenwise** quant (fewer scale bytes); salient-token identification via normalized attention scores | FlashAttention-compatible; saliency decoupled from full scores | KV 4.98× ↓ @ −0.38% (Mistral GSM8k); decode-phase latency **−56.9%** |
| **2503.18773 BitDecoding** | 2/4-bit, tensor- & channel-wise scales; NVFP4/MXFP4 native on Blackwell | See Q2: TC + warp-dequant software pipeline, layout-preserving pack | 4.8× Ampere / 7.5× avg vs FP16 FlashDecoding-v2; 3× @128K batch-1 |
| **2411.10958 SageAttention2** | Q,K→INT4 (per-thread) / INT8; P̃,V→FP8 (two-level accumulation; fp8-mma accumulator is FP22) | INT4/INT8 mma for QKᵀ; FP8 mma for P̃V on Ada/Hopper; scales outside mma; Q-smoothing (subtract channel mean of Q, add Q̄·K back) | 3× FA2 on 4090 (481 TOPS); matches FA3(fp8) on Hopper w/ better accuracy |
| **2606.06521 P-Cast** (sibling brief) | fp8 e4m3 P | S=2⁸ bit-exact cast, reverse KV iteration (attention-sink underflow), ℓ fp32 pre-cast | precision recipe for fp8-P |
| **2602.10718 SnapMLA** (sibling brief) | fp8 MLA decode | descale pipeline scheduled across KV loop (consumer-side cvt) | Hopper; pattern transfers |

**Format decision for us:** Ampere (sm_87) has **no fp8/fp6 TC datapath and no IMMA for fp8** — so any fp8/fp6 KV decode *must* be dequant→bf16/fp16 MMA (or INT8 IMMA if codes were int). That is exactly the structure all three leading papers converge on for sub-8-bit KV on pre-Hopper silicon: **dequant in registers/smem on CUDA cores, TCs do bf16/fp16 mma, scales applied outside the inner loop** (K-scales folded into Q via diag(s)·k̂ ⇒ S=(Q∘s)·K̂ᵀ; V per-token scales onto the PV accumulator epilogue). BitDecoding's extra trick — pack codes *in the ldmatrix fragment layout* so dequant needs no shuffle — is directly implementable for e2m3/fp8 HND pages.

**Negative results:** `KIVAR` does not exist on arXiv (searched ti:/abs:; only unrelated homonyms). `FP8-LC` does not exist on arXiv — the fp8-KV line is: FA3 (2407.08608, Hopper), vLLM PagedAttention (**2309.06180**, paged KV management; fp8 KV is an engineering feature with per-tensor/per-head scales, no dedicated paper), P-Cast, SnapMLA. "SmoothAttention" standalone = **§4.2 of QServe** (Lin et al.) — not a separate paper.

## Q4 — Orin/Jetson decode measurements

- **2506.09554 — Understanding the Performance and Power of LLM Inferencing on Edge Accelerators** (Arya & Simmhan, 2025): AGX Orin 64GB characterization (Llama3.1/Phi2/DeepSeek-R1-Qwen, 2.7–32.8B): token throughput **falls with sequence length** (KV traffic), quantization effects non-monotonic at small scale, power-mode caps throttle decode → benchmark at MAXN/pinned clocks. System-level only; no kernel GB/s.
- **2412.15352 — LLMs on Small Resource-Constrained Systems** (Seymour, Kutukcu, Baidya, 2024): Orin-family backend deltas on decode speed; harness.
- **2505.23219 — Ghidorah** (Wei et al., 2025): Jetson NX-class unified memory: spec-decode + hetero-core split, **7.6× decode speedup**; confirms unified-memory bus is the contention point — keep drafter/CPU off the LPDDR bus during GPU verify steps.
- **2609.08307 — Edge-continuum measurement study** (Khatib et al., 2026): self-hosted serving across edge hardware; quality/latency/energy tradeoffs. Marginal for kernel work.
- **2601.10953 — SwiftKV** (Zhang et al., 2026): edge *ASIC* attention: single-pass per-token pipeline (no score materialization, no blockwise second pass) + fp GEMV/attention fused accelerator; 7.16–13.48× attention speedup on their hardware. Useful concept (per-token steady-state pipeline, single pass) but not a Jetson GPU result.
- **Negative result (confirmed this run):** no arXiv paper reports kernel-level achieved GB/s (vs roofline) for decode attention on AGX Orin or any Jetson. Any Orin achieved-BW number we publish is novel measurement; use the gate + same-run ratios per repo discipline. Orin roofline for our shape: L=128K, 4 KV heads, hd256, fp8 = 128 MB/step/layer → **625 µs at 100% of 204.8 GB/s; ~830 µs at 75%**. Current 18 GB/s → ~7.1 ms/layer/step (≈11× off).

## Q5 — Low-rank / shared-structure tricks for GQA decode

- **2405.04434 — DeepSeek-V2 (MLA).** Low-rank joint KV compression: cache is one `c_KV` latent (512+64 dims) instead of per-head K,V; KV cache −93.3%, max generation throughput 5.76×; *"MLA is also optimized based on an improved version of FlashAttention-2"* (fused TC decode kernel; matrix-absorbed form means one latent read per token serves all heads — the ultimate "amortize loads across query heads").
- **2507.15465 — Rethinking Bottlenecks:** MLA's core-attention ArI is **>2 orders of magnitude above MHA** → compute-bound at the ridge point regardless of batch (with layer reordering); attention-specialized hardware (PIM) becomes unnecessary. The model-level endgame for the bandwidth problem.
- **2502.07864 TransMLA / 2610.17202 MLAfication:** post-hoc **GQA→MLA conversion** (QKNorm-compatible, parameter-efficient distillation). MLAfication on Qwen3-30B-A3B: **5.35× serving throughput vs BF16 GQA**, and with 4-bit KV −94.73% KV bytes. This is the actionable route if the served GQA 24:4 model can be converted+calibrated.
- **2501.06425 — TPA (Tensor Product Attention):** factorize Q/K/V into contextual low-rank TPR tensors (rank r), RoPE-compatible; KV cache shrinks with r. Method paper; decode-kernel implications analogous to MLA.
- **2603.02188 — MLRA:** multi-head *partitionable* latents (fixes MLA's TP-sharding flaw); 2.8× decode vs MLA.
- **2610.20686 — RoLaQ:** 2-bit quantized MLA latents (rotation+range calibration): 3.96× BF16-KV capacity, **2.51× decode throughput** (SGLang, DeepSeek-V2-Lite, 256K).
- **Approximate KV-byte reducers (no retraining, kernel-compatible):** **2406.10774 Quest** — per-page min/max K stats + query-aware top-K page loading, 2.23× self-attention speedup (needs a scoring pass + indirection); **2410.10819 DuoAttention** — retrieval vs streaming heads: streaming heads keep only sink+window KV → GQA **1.50× decode, 1.67× memory** (needs per-head calibration; changes semantics); **2410.00161 KV-Compress** — per-head variable-rate paged compression (vLLM-compatible); **2510.07486 AsyncSpade** — async query-aware page selection for TTS; **2412.14468 HashAttention** — semantic-sparsity bit signatures.

**Applicability verdict:** MLA/TPA-class tricks change the model — out of scope for serving an existing GQA 24:4 checkpoint unless a conversion (TransMLA/MLAfication) is acceptable. Quest/DuoAttention are drop-in *approximations* that cut bytes 2–5× but trade accuracy/complexity; on Orin they'd multiply the win of a bandwidth-saturated kernel (bytes↓ × BW↑). First fix the kernel; only then consider byte reduction.

## Ranked actions for sm_87 hd256 GQA 24:4 paged fp8/fp6 decode (18 → >100 GB/s)

1. **Token-parallel split-KV with equalized bytes/SM (LeanAttention stream-K + FlashDecoding++ async softmax + FlashInfer ⊕).** ~32 CTAs (2/SM × 16), partials pinned ≈ 2×#SMs, unequal chunk sizes to fill the wave; balanced-tree LSE merge. Directly attacks the 8.3% occupancy. Expected: 3–6×.
2. **Dequant-fused TC inner loop (BitDecoding + SageAttention + KIVI).** Pack fp8/fp6 codes in ldmatrix fragment layout; consumer-stage `cvt` fp8→fp16 (fold per-channel K scales into Q once per step; per-token V scale on PV epilogue); bf16 `mma.m16n8k16` with the 6 Q rows padded to 8 (FlashDecoding++ pad-to-8; 25% lane waste vs >87% at M=64 padding). Removes the scalar-FMA issue bottleneck that caps loads-in-flight at 8.3% occupancy. Expected: 2–4× (compounding with #1).
3. **Deep cp.async ring sized to 100 KB smem** (FlashDecoding++/LeanTile byte budget; BitDecoding `cp.async.ca` for fine-grained packed codes): 32–64 fp8 KV rows/stage → 3-stage K+V ring ≈ 48 KB + Q + stats; wide contiguous 256 B rows (HND pages keep K rows dense on Orin — fp8 avoids the 64 B-request wall we hit with packed fp6 on Thor). Hides LPDDR5 latency; prerequisite for #1/#2 to reach >50% of peak. Expected: 1.5–2×.
4. **M-shape policy:** M=1: keep a GEMV path only if a profiled high-occupancy split shows it winning (FlashDecoding++: GEMV +22% at M=1 on A100); M≥2 (spec verify): TC path. Never re-tile per M — one m16 tile serves M=1..8.
5. **Optional byte-cut (accuracy tradeoff):** DuoAttention streaming-heads (1.5× decode on GQA) or Quest page selection (2.23×), only after #1–3 land; multiplies with achieved BW.
6. **Structural (out of scope unless model conversion allowed):** GQA→MLA (MLAfication/TransMLA, 5.35× serving claim; MLA decode is compute-bound — the bandwidth problem disappears).

**Explicit TC-vs-GEMV claims (asked for):** BitDecoding 2503.18773 (TC decode ≫ CUDA-core decode for GQA low-bit KV; 4.8× on Ampere) and FlashDecoding++ 2311.01282 (TC flat-GEMM vs GEMV crossover between M=1 and M=4; M=1 GEMV +22%) are the two paper-level datapoints. QServe 2405.04532 supplies the cost model (1 CUDA op ≈ 50 TC ops on A100) explaining why dequant-on-CUDA-cores must be minimized but can ride free when the kernel is bandwidth-bound.
